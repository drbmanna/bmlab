Research

Computational approaches to stress biology, evolution, and molecular function.

Oxidative Stress Biology

Reactive oxygen species (ROS) are inevitable byproducts of aerobic metabolism and environmental stressors. We build computational models of how microbial cells sense, signal, and recover from oxidative damage — integrating protein-level structural changes with network-level regulatory responses. Current focus includes thiol-based redox switches, Hsp70 chaperone dynamics under oxidative load, and transcriptional rewiring in E. coli and S. cerevisiae.

Antimicrobial Resistance Evolution

Antimicrobial resistance is one of the defining public health challenges of this century. We use comparative genomics, phylogenomics, and evolutionary modelling to understand how resistance determinants emerge, spread, and persist in bacterial populations. We focus on WHO-priority pathogens and track resistance across clinical isolates, livestock microbiomes, and wastewater — asking which genetic and ecological factors most accelerate spread.

Multi-omics Integration

No single omics layer tells the full story. We develop and apply computational pipelines that integrate transcriptomic, proteomic, and metabolomic data to reconstruct condition-specific regulatory programs. A core commitment is reproducibility: all pipelines are version-controlled, containerised, and benchmarked on public datasets before application to new experimental data. We collaborate with wet-lab groups to co-design experiments that maximise multi-omics interpretability.

Molecular Dynamics Simulations

Oxidative post-translational modifications — disulfide formation, methionine oxidation, carbonylation — can profoundly alter protein function. We use all-atom and coarse-grained molecular dynamics to characterise these structural consequences at atomic resolution, with a focus on redox-sensitive enzymes and chaperones. Long-timescale simulations are run on HPC clusters; analysis pipelines are written in Python using MDAnalysis and MDTraj.
